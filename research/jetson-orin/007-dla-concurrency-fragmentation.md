# 007 — What actually limits DLA concurrency: not bandwidth, but how fragmented the engine is

**Platform:** Turing Pi 2.5; node2 Jetson Orin NX 16GB (2 NVDLA v2 engines), JetPack 7.2 /
L4T R39.2 / CUDA 13.2.
**Software:** TensorRT **11.3.0.99** (`[b99]`), side-by-side in `~/dla-trt11`. The system
TensorRT 10.16.2.10 reports `num_DLA_cores = 0` and cannot target DLA at all — see
[003 §2](003-dla-transformer-offload.md).
**Date:** 2026-11-24 (§3b same day). **Status:** complete. Measurements in `selfmodel.db` run `eec630726c24`.

## Abstract

Two engines, two prior explanations, both wrong. [003 §6b](003-dla-transformer-offload.md)
attributed the DLA's failure to scale to **DRAM bandwidth saturation**; [003 §6c](003-dla-transformer-offload.md),
written earlier today, attributed it to a **shared ~253 qps ceiling**. A controlled pair of models
— identical convolution stacks differing by exactly one operator — refutes both. A strict-DLA
model with zero GPU layers reaches **1133.8 qps aggregate across the two engines, 4.5x the claimed
"ceiling"**, while using **4.6% of peak DRAM bandwidth**. Adding a single GPU-resident elementwise
op costs 14.7% of single-engine throughput and drops two-engine scaling from 1.806x to 1.628x.
Across four engines of increasing fragmentation the scaling factor falls monotonically from 1.806x
to 1.034x. The limit is **how much of the model is not on the DLA**, and the two-engine ceiling
claimed this morning does not exist.

## 1. Retraction

[003 §6c](003-dla-transformer-offload.md), committed in `2672bf1` earlier on 2026-11-24, states:

> "Shared ceiling ~253 qps and both precisions run into it: fp16 approaches it with two engines,
> INT8 reaches it with one."

**This is withdrawn.** 252.88 qps was a property of that particular ResNet-18 INT8 engine, not of
the hardware. The same two engines reach 1133.8 qps on a model that stays on the DLA — 4.5x
higher. The error was inferring a hardware ceiling from two variants of one model, which is the
same mistake as inferring a concurrency law from a single engine. Capability rows A27 and A30 are
corrected accordingly.

The INT8 result itself stands: that engine really does stop scaling (1.034x). The mistake was
naming the cause.

## 2. Method

Two ONNX models generated by [`gen_models.py`](../../capability-matrix/scripts/bench/dla/gen_models.py),
identical except for one operator:

| | `conv_pure` | `conv_fallback` |
|---|---|---|
| Body | 8x (Conv 3x3 64->64 + ReLU) | the same 8x |
| Extra | — | one residual `Add` |
| Weights | 0.590 MB | 0.590 MB |
| Activation | 0.401 MB | 0.401 MB |
| DLA build | **strict** (no `--allowGPUFallback`) | requires `--allowGPUFallback` |
| GPU layers in engine | **zero, by construction** | exactly one (`ELEMENTWISE`) |

The strict build is the control that matters: TensorRT refuses to build a strict DLA engine if any
layer needs the GPU, so a strict engine that compiles is *proof* of zero GPU participation. DLA
requires `chw16` I/O for this (`--inputIOFormats=chw16 --outputIOFormats=chw16`); note that
TensorRT 11.3 takes the bare format name, not `fp16:chw16`, because it is strongly typed.

Both weights and activations are tiny on purpose, so neither model can be bandwidth-bound and
bandwidth is removed as a variable rather than argued away.

60 s per arm, `--avgRuns=100`, `--warmUp=2000`, two concurrent `trtexec` processes for the 2-engine
arms (one per core), rails sampled in-process at 1 s. Module otherwise quiet: the live dashboard
stopped and the probe cron paused, verified at GPU load 0 and 8.25 W idle before starting.

## 3. Results

| Arm | Agg qps | Per engine | mean ms | VDD_IN | inf/J | DRAM | % of 102.4 GB/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pure` 1 engine | 627.73 | 627.73 | 1.592 | 15.21 W | 41.3 | 2.64 GB/s | 2.6% |
| `pure` 2 engines | **1133.80** | 566.90 | 1.763 | 20.27 W | **55.9** | 4.76 GB/s | **4.6%** |
| `fallback` 1 engine | 535.52 | 535.52 | 1.866 | 15.50 W | 34.6 | 2.25 GB/s | 2.2% |
| `fallback` 2 engines | 871.73 | 435.87 | 2.293 | 19.64 W | 44.4 | 3.66 GB/s | 3.6% |

Scaling: **`pure` 1.806x, `fallback` 1.628x.** Power sd <= 0.22 W over 57 samples per arm; the two
engines agree to within 0.001 qps in both 2-engine arms.

DRAM figures count weights plus all nine activation tensors per inference, which overstates real
traffic.

### Finding 1 — it is not bandwidth

The fastest arm uses **4.6% of peak DRAM bandwidth**. 003 §6b's workload read 83.89 MB per
inference and genuinely did saturate DRAM at 97% of peak, and its conclusion was correct *for that
workload*. It does not generalise, and §6b's unqualified sentence "the two DLA engines do not
scale with each other" should be read as bandwidth-bound-only.

### Finding 2 — one GPU operator costs 14.7%

`conv_fallback` differs from `conv_pure` by a single elementwise add. That one operator costs
**92.2 qps of single-engine throughput (-14.7%)** and **0.274 ms of per-inference latency**, and
degrades two-engine scaling from 1.806x to 1.628x. The cost is not the add's arithmetic — it is
one DLA->GPU->DLA round trip per inference.

### Finding 3 — scaling falls monotonically with fragmentation

Fragmentation measured as the number of separate DLA `ForeignNode`s TensorRT emits; more nodes
means more DLA/GPU boundary crossings per inference.

| Engine | DLA nodes | GPU ops | Two-engine scaling |
|---|---|---|---|
| `conv_pure` | 1 | 0 | **1.806x** |
| `conv_fallback` | 1 | 1 | 1.628x |
| ResNet-18 fp16 | 9 | many (residual adds, flatten, matmul) | 1.601x |
| ResNet-18 INT8 | 16 | most (70 Quantize / 110 Dequantize) | **1.034x** |

The ordering is monotonic across a 4.5x range of absolute throughput. **How much of the model
leaves the DLA predicts how well the two engines scale**, and it predicts it better than either
bandwidth or any fixed qps ceiling.

### Finding 4 — even pure DLA does not reach 2.0x

1.806x, not 2.0x, with zero GPU involvement and 4.6% of bandwidth in use. So a third limit exists
beyond fallback and bandwidth — plausibly the shared DLA DMA path, shared SRAM, or driver submit
serialisation. **Not isolated here.** This is the honest residual: fragmentation explains the
spread between engines, not the last 10%.

### Finding 5 — P7's boundary condition holds

Energy per unit work improves with concurrency wherever concurrency buys throughput: `pure`
41.3 -> 55.9 inf/J (**+35%**), `fallback` 34.6 -> 44.4 (**+28%**). Combined with
[003 §6c](003-dla-transformer-offload.md), where the INT8 engine *lost* 8% because it gained only
3% throughput, the rule is confirmed in both directions on the same hardware: concurrency lowers
energy per unit work exactly while it still converts into throughput.

## 3b. Isolated: a fixed ~0.21 ms per-submit serialization (2026-11-24)

Finding 4 left 1.806x-vs-2.0x unexplained. It is now isolated, by varying DLA-resident work per
submit while holding the submit count at exactly one (strict DLA, one fused `ForeignNode`). Two
mechanisms predict opposite things: a **fixed per-submit cost** means the serialized share shrinks
as the model deepens, so scaling must rise toward 2.0x; **proportional contention** in a shared
datapath means scaling stays flat. Measurements in `selfmodel.db` run `9b15b7dfbaec`.

| convs | 1 engine | 2 engines | scaling | implied serialized fraction | per-engine latency, 1 -> 2 | adder |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2242.90 qps (0.444 ms) | 3120.11 | **1.391x** | 0.438 | 0.4445 -> 0.6396 ms | +0.195 ms |
| 8 | 627.55 qps (1.592 ms) | 1143.16 | **1.822x** | 0.098 | 1.5921 -> 1.7480 ms | +0.156 ms |
| 32 | 163.28 qps (6.123 ms) | 313.04 | **1.917x** | 0.043 | 6.1231 -> 6.3872 ms | +0.264 ms |

**Scaling rises monotonically with depth, so the cost is fixed per submit, not proportional.**
Activating the second engine adds a flat **0.21 +- 0.05 ms** to every inference, whether that
inference takes 0.44 ms or 6.12 ms — constant across a 13.8x range of inference time, which a
proportional mechanism cannot produce. Fitting `S = 2/(1 + 0.205/T)` reproduces all three measured
scalings within 3%. The depth-8 arm also replicates §3's 1.806x at 1.822x (0.9%).

### Three mechanisms ruled out by measurement, not argument

- **DVFS / power budget.** Both `dla0_core` and `dla1_core` held **1228.8 MHz = `max_rate`** in
  every arm, with mean equal to minimum, and EMC held 3199 MHz. The module was in `MAXN_SUPER`.
  Clock reduction cannot explain any part of the deficit.
- **CPU-side interrupt servicing.** CPU0 never exceeded **20.7% busy** (irq <= 2.27%,
  softirq <= 0.14%) in any arm. Moreover the `host1x_syncpt` interrupt counter showed **zero
  delta** during every arm, so DLA completions do not arrive on that IRQ at all. A prior
  hypothesis — that all DLA completions serialize on one `host1x_syncpt` IRQ pinned to CPU0,
  which `/proc/interrupts` does show carrying 1.2M counts exclusively on CPU0 — is **refuted**:
  that IRQ belongs to other host1x clients.
- **DRAM bandwidth.** Already excluded in §3 at 4.6% of peak; the deepest model here moves even
  less per unit time.

### What remains

A fixed serialized segment of ~0.21 ms per inference, inside the DLA submit/completion path,
that is paid only when the second engine is active. It is not the clocks, not CPU interrupt
servicing, and not memory bandwidth. Distinguishing the remaining candidates — a single driver
submit lock, a shared firmware channel in the DLA falcon, or a shared internal SRAM/DMA
arbitration step — needs driver-level tracing (`ftrace` on `nvhost`, or NVIDIA's own DLA
profiling), which is not available from userspace here. **Named, bounded and quantified, but the
specific shared resource is still not identified.**

### Practical rule

The second DLA engine is worth almost nothing on short inferences and almost everything on long
ones: **1.39x at 0.44 ms, 1.92x at 6.12 ms.** Below roughly 2 ms of DLA-resident work per
inference, the second engine returns less than half of what it costs in added latency. This, not
TOPS, is the number to design around — and it compounds with §3's fragmentation result, since
fallback both lengthens and multiplies submits.

## 4. Consequences

- **For anyone offloading to DLA:** the headline number to optimise is not TOPS or precision, it
  is the count of layers that fall back. One elementwise add cost more here (-14.7%) than the
  difference between one and two engines is worth in several configurations.
- **For 003's verdict:** unchanged. Even at 1133.8 qps the GPU does 1360.3 qps on ResNet-18 while
  the DLA's best ResNet-18 arm is 252.88, and the GPU still wins on energy. Nothing here makes a
  ggml DLA backend attractive.
- **For P8:** the "one ceiling" framing needs care. There is no single hardware ceiling the
  engines share; there is a per-engine limit set by that engine's own non-DLA content. P8 should
  cite this rather than the withdrawn 253 figure.

## 5. Limitations

- One carrier, one module, one DLA generation (NVDLA v2). Nothing here is checked on Orin AGX or
  on Xavier, and the Orin Nano has no DLA at all.
- Fragmentation is a *correlate* measured across four engines, not a controlled sweep. The
  controlled part is `pure` vs `fallback` (one operator, everything else identical); the ResNet-18
  rows differ in many ways at once and only corroborate the ordering.
- Finding 4's residual is now isolated to a fixed ~0.21 ms per-submit serialization (§3b), with DVFS, CPU/IRQ servicing and bandwidth each excluded by measurement. The specific shared resource inside the submit path is still unidentified and needs driver-level tracing.
- `conv_pure`/`conv_fallback` are synthetic. They were designed to remove bandwidth as a variable,
  which also makes them unrepresentative of real CNNs.
- Power is module-level VDD_IN; carrier fan/BMC/switch excluded. `llama-server` stayed resident
  but idle in every arm, so absolute watts include it and deltas do not.
- Random weights; no accuracy was computed and none is claimed.

## 6. Reproduction

```sh
# on node2 (Orin NX), with the live dashboard stopped and the probe cron paused
cd ~/dla-trt11
./qvenv/bin/python gen_models.py     # conv_pure.onnx, conv_fallback.onnx
python3 ceiling.py                   # 4 arms, rails sampled in-process
```

`ceiling.py` prints each arm as one JSON line plus the two scaling factors, and writes
`ceiling.json`. Raw output is committed at
[`capability-matrix/data/dla/ceiling.json`](../../capability-matrix/data/dla/ceiling.json).
